## What it is

A fully local RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) system that lets you ask questions against your
Joplin notes and get answers from an LLM running on your own hardware. No API keys, no cloud, no
data leaving your device.

## Why I built it

A friend of mine had shown me how they use a RAG at his job for searching through presentations,
documents, etc and can then ask the chat something like "what was the presentation from July 1st about".

I thought this was great because I've obssessed over all my hobbies, projects and interests and used
to create txt files to store information about them (a niche link that would fix a rogue Linux error on my laptop, etc). 

Luckily, over the years, I've gotten more sophisticated and moved over to using Joplin to store my notes.
However, the problem is that the notes are incredibly long. They're organized *enough* and ultimately if I
need to directly search through them, I *can* find what I am trying to find. However, search only works when you
remember the exact words you used. I wanted to ask questions in plain English and get answers with
sources, without handing my notes to OpenAI to do it.

## How it works

My PC/Laptop have the Joplin app installed on them, so I have access to the Joplin SQLite DB directly.


Thus:

```
Joplin SQLite DB
      │
      ▼
  ingest.py  ── sentence-transformers ──► ChromaDB (vector index)
                                                │
                                          similarity search
                                                │
  Your question ─────────────────────► relevant note chunks
                                                │
                                                ▼
                                         Ollama (local LLM)
                                                │
                                                ▼
                                        Answer + sources cited
```

**Ingest** — reads directly from Joplin's SQLite database (no Joplin API, no export step), splits
notes into overlapping chunks, embeds them with `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, and stores them in ChromaDB.

**Query** — your question gets embedded the same way, ChromaDB finds the most relevant chunks by
cosine similarity, and those chunks are passed as context to the LLM.

**Answer** — the LLM answers grounded in your notes and cites which notes it drew from.

## Stack

| Component | Technology |
|---|---|
| LLM | Ollama — runs models locally |
| Embeddings | `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` |
| Vector DB | ChromaDB |
| UI | Gradio |
| Notes source | Joplin SQLite (read-only, direct DB access) |

Runs on AMD (ROCm), Nvidia (CUDA), or CPU. Default model is `qwen2.5:14b` — sized for 8–16 GB VRAM,
swappable in `.env`.

## Why it works (just for me)

Currently, I don't have a server with a GPU that runs 24/7. As you might see in my server stack, my
"GPU" for encoding videos is just the IGPU on an intel CPU. Therefore, I need this solution (as flawed
as it might be) to be fast enough to run either on my laptop or my PC (without outside compute). This is why
I opted for the lightweight ollama model which might get things wrong, but it will point me towards
the right sections of the notes, etc.

I could use something like [Zo Computer](https://www.zo.computer/) (shameless plug for the startup my friend works for),
to take in my notes and then ask it about them for sure. However, then I surrender my notes to a 3rd party 
and I just appreciate having control over things.

## What I'd do differently

Full rebuild on every ingest is fine for a personal notes collection but doesn't scale. A proper
incremental ingest. I.e: tracking note modification timestamps, only re-embed changed notes.

This would make re-indexing instant rather than a couple of minutes.
