## What it is

An Android app that blocks distracting apps when you tap an NFC tag — and keeps them blocked until
you tap the tag again. There is no in-app stop button. The only way out is the tag.

It runs entirely without Google Play Services and **can** work on de-Googled ROMs like GrapheneOS and
CalyxOS. Currently, I run it on a daily basis on a regular Pixel phone with regular Android OS.

## Why I built it

Every screen time app I tried had the same flaw: a way to turn it off that required no more friction
than opening the app you were trying to avoid. The whole point is that willpower fails. I wanted a
physical barrier — something you have to get up and walk to. An NFC tag stuck to a wall across the
room is that barrier.

Obviously, the company [Brick](https://getbrick.com/) exists. The problem is that their 
[privacy policy](https://getbrick.com/pages/privacy-policy) sucks. To be honest, this is a flaw
with damn near any closed-source app and I still use my banking apps, etc. However, I figure phone 
usage data is quite sensitive, so I would definitely rather keep that private.

I might make general post about my overarching beliefs on privacy and making apps around it, but yeah. That 
previous paragraph is a brief glimpse I guess.

Also, a brick is 45 dollars.
It's a glorified piece of plastic with an NFC tag hidden inside. Come on.

## How it works

```
Tap NFC tag
    │
    ▼
Android NDEF_DISCOVERED intent → MainActivity
    │  extracts lockout:profile:<uuid> from tag payload
    ▼
Flutter picks up the pending tag on resume
    │  looks up profile in local storage
    ▼
BlockingChannel (MethodChannel)
    │  writes package list to native SharedPreferences
    ▼
AccessibilityService (Kotlin)
    │  TYPE_WINDOW_STATE_CHANGED fires on every foreground change
    ▼
Blocked app in foreground → send home instantly
```

The tag payload is just a UUID — 36 bytes, fits any NTAG213 tag (~$0.50) with room to spare. The
profile lives in app storage. You can rename or edit a profile without rewriting the tag.

## Key decisions

**AccessibilityService, not UsageStatsManager** — UsageStatsManager requires polling, which burns
battery and has coarse timing. `TYPE_WINDOW_STATE_CHANGED` fires synchronously: zero polling,
sub-100ms response.

**Send home, not force-stop** — Apps can't force-stop other apps without root. Sending `ACTION_MAIN
/ CATEGORY_HOME` is instant, reliable, and doesn't corrupt the blocked app's state.

**Dual SharedPreferences (Flutter + native)** — If an OEM battery saver kills and restarts the
AccessibilityService without relaunching Flutter, the service reads the block list from native prefs
in `onServiceConnected`. Flutter prefs handle the human-visible session state. The blocking survives
app kills.

**No internet permission** — Declared in the manifest and intentionally permanent. No analytics, no
telemetry, no crash reporting. Ever.

## Stack

- **Flutter** — UI and profile management
- **Kotlin** — AccessibilityService, NFC dispatch, native prefs bridge
- **flutter_nfc_kit** — tag read/write

## What's next

Work Profile DPC blocking via `DevicePolicyManager` for Samsung and Xiaomi devices with aggressive
battery savers that kill the AccessibilityService before it can react.

If you block notifications for an app while the phone is "Locked Out" then when it is unlocked,
those notifications do not return. I have unfortunately missed a few Whatsapp messages this way. Therefore,
a notification summary thing is absolutely necessary.
